Workshop on Quantum Metrology in Many-body and Open Systems

Barcelona (UAB), April 12–16, 2027

4th edition

About

QuMIMOS brings together researchers working on quantum sensing with interacting and open quantum systems.

Interactions and dissipation are often seen as obstacles for quantum sensors, but they also open new possibilities. Many-body interactions produce correlated states and collective dynamics that can serve as resources for quantum-enhanced estimation, for example near quantum phase transitions or in driven-dissipative systems. They also lead to estimation tasks beyond phase estimation, such as measuring a temperature, the parameters of a non-equilibrium steady state, or an unknown Hamiltonian. These questions connect quantum metrology with many-body physics, open quantum systems, thermodynamics and quantum learning.

The workshop gathers researchers from these communities to present recent results, discuss open problems and start new collaborations. Topics include:

  • Fundamental precision limits under noise, and error correction and control strategies to approach them
  • Critical and many-body enhanced sensing, in and out of equilibrium
  • Continuous monitoring, sequential measurements and sensing with driven-dissipative systems
  • Quantum thermometry and the thermodynamics of measurement
  • Hamiltonian learning and the characterisation of many-body systems
  • Multiparameter estimation, quantum imaging and links with quantum learning theory
  • Distributed sensing with networks of quantum sensors

Early-career researchers are encouraged to participate and present their work.

The 2027 edition is hosted by the Quantum Information Group (GIQ) of the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona at the Facultat de Ciències, Bellaterra.

Format

  • Talks: invited and contributed talks, with time for discussion.
  • Poster session: participants present posters on their recent work.
  • Tutorials: the first day opens with introductory tutorials on quantum metrology, also covering topics such as continuous monitoring and many-body metrology.
